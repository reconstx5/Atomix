# Let the artwork lead and keep glass on the chrome

The best media apps of 2025–2026 look alike, and they do not look like the most glass-heavy concept shots. They put one large, focus-driven spotlight at the top: the backdrop, a clear logo, a single line of metadata and a short synopsis over a scrim. Below it run dense rows of 2:3 "theatrical" posters, about six per TV row, with short text badges such as "New Season" and "#1 in Shows". Glass is kept for chrome that floats over moving pictures, such as player controls, docks and menus. Apple's Liquid Glass showed what happens when text sits on translucency: Apple had to add tint presets and then, in iOS 27, a transparency slider. Netflix's 2025 TV redesign drew complaints that it showed fewer titles, and in November 2026 Netflix began testing a collapsed left icon rail. Plex was punished on TV for replacing its pinned-library sidebar. Self-hosted users ask for accent colours with exact hex values, an OLED-black option, a sidebar they can keep, and themes that do not break. Much of this is already in Orbit's design: a floating menu that grows on focus, a spotlight that follows focus, 2:3 and 16:9 cards, colour taken from the artwork, and a Reduce effects switch. What Orbit still lacks is mostly a matter of numbers: a type floor of at least 20 px at 1080p, a glass setting with more than two steps, eased scrims, a fixed box for the logo, and off-white text. The bigger gain is cross-theme. A layered token system with contrast checks would let every theme take a safe accent, an OLED, high-contrast or large-text modifier, and any layout. No Plex, Jellyfin or Emby client appears to offer that set today.

## The streaming giants converged on one spotlight, dense posters and text badges

All the 2025–26 redesigns share one structure. Apple's TV app in tvOS 26 and iOS 26 replaced horizontal artwork with **vertical poster art described as more "theatrical"**, and reviewers noted that "content density has improved quite a bit" ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/07/16/apples-tv-app-gets-fresh-design-in-ios-26-and-tvos-26-heres-whats-new/)). Disney+ now uses "cinematic, poster-style artwork", a top carousel that plays video, and badges such as "Season Finales" and "New Series" ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/entertainment/disney-plus/disney-plus-gets-a-major-redesign-heres-whats-changing)). Prime Video's hero rotator autoplays video on living-room devices ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/prime-video-gets-a-much-needed-ui-overhaul-with-a-new-content-bar-and-ai-recommendations-120019397.html)). Netflix's May 2025 TV layout lets users preview "synopsis, runtime, award wins, Top 10 history, or key cast all up-front while you browse" ([Netflix Tudum](https://www.netflix.com/tudum/articles/netflix-new-tv-layout)). It leads each row with a large card that updates in place as focus moves, without opening the title's page ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/05/07/netflix-redesigned-tv-experience/)).

Netflix also shows what that format costs. The large tiles and top bar drew mostly critical early feedback ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/netflix-new-tv-experience-redesign-3555283)). In a test that began on **3 November 2026**, Netflix moved the menu back to the left as a slim icon rail that expands into a panel on focus, put large video of the highlighted title near the top with the logo, genre, season count, rating and synopsis overlaid, and showed **six tiles per row** because the current design "drew criticism for showing fewer titles" ([What's on Netflix](https://www.whats-on-netflix.com/news/netflix-tv-app-redesign-test-sidebar-menu/)). This is only a test, but it comes from the most data-driven company in the field. It suggests that the premium look is one rich spotlight plus dense, quiet rows, not rows of large, rich tiles. Card metadata has moved the same way: contextual words ("#1 in TV Shows", "Emmy Award Winner", "New Season") have replaced generic chrome badges ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/netflix-new-tv-experience-redesign-3555283)).

Detail pages follow the same artwork-first approach. Plex's redesign added more artwork to detail and cast pages and showed title logos "where available", which it called "a long-requested feature" ([Plex blog](https://plex.tv/blog/the-new-plex-experience)). Infuse 8's users showed how this can go wrong. When a slow server delays the logo, the layout jumps down once it arrives, and the new episode view hides metadata while the user scrolls ([Firecore community](https://community.firecore.com/t/infuse-8-now-available/51308.md)). Clear logos are standard because transparent PNG and WebP logos adapt to any background ([Smashing Magazine](https://smashingmagazine.com/2025/09/designing-tv-principles-patterns-practical-guidance/)). They only look polished when their space is reserved before they load.

Coverage has gaps. The sources gave little 2025–26 visual detail for Max, Google TV, YouTube TV, Infuse's home screen or Channels DVR, and no teardown with measured tile sizes. Atomix's relevant point is that the Orbit design already follows the convergent pattern. Its spotlight follows focus after a 300 ms pause, it uses 2:3 posters without captions in discovery rows and 16:9 cards with progress for Continue Watching, and its floating menu grows to show names. The improvements below are refinements to that design, not a change of direction.

## Glass works on floating chrome and fails under text

Apple's Liquid Glass is the main experiment with glass in this period, and its results are mixed. On TV the effect is "less pronounced" and appears mostly in Control Center, the Home Screen dock and the scrub bar ([AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/25/09/14/tvos-26-review-high-on-polish-light-on-new-features-for-apple-tv)). The most praised use is **player controls that float over the video instead of covering it** ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/07/16/apples-tv-app-gets-fresh-design-in-ios-26-and-tvos-26-heres-whats-new/)). On iOS, beta users said translucent elements made text "harder to read" and that toggle states were hard to tell apart ([Cult of Mac](https://www.cultofmac.com/news/readability-concerns-take-shine-out-of-apples-new-liquid-glass-aesthetic)). One practitioner reports early screens measuring about **1.5:1 contrast** ([BuildMVPFast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/liquid-glass-css-backdrop-filter-recipes-2026)). Apple first offered Clear and Tinted presets. In the iOS 27 beta it replaced them with a **continuous slider from transparency to tint**, because the presets "left many users wanting more control" ([Tom's Guide](https://tomsguide.com/phones/iphones/ios-27-has-a-hidden-liquid-glass-slider-here-is-how-to-find-and-use-it)). Liquid Glass also skips older Apple TV hardware ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/06/09/tvos-26-liquid-glass-redesign-older-models/)), which suggests that real-time blur is expensive on TV-class GPUs.

The web numbers for glass are consistent across sources. A typical 2026 recipe uses a white fill at 0.12 alpha, `blur(16px) saturate(180%)`, a 1 px border at 0.28 alpha with a brighter top edge, and a soft shadow. For large surfaces the blur should stay under about 20 px, with **no more than three or four glass layers per viewport** and the blur radius never animated. The fallback chain runs from a solid surface at about 0.85 alpha, to glass inside `@supports`, to a 0.92-alpha card under reduced transparency, to a 2 px `currentColor` border under `prefers-contrast: more` ([BuildMVPFast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/liquid-glass-css-backdrop-filter-recipes-2026)). `prefers-reduced-transparency` follows the operating system setting. The recommended pattern makes surfaces opaque by default and lowers opacity only inside `no-preference` ([engineered.at](https://engineered.at/articles/day-103-the-prefers-reduced-transparency-media-feature)).

The spotlight has a more basic problem: text set over artwork. A simple `linear-gradient(to top, rgba(0,0,0,.85), transparent)` falls short. The scrim must be taller than the text, and the robust test is to put **pure white behind the scrim** and measure the contrast. Ahmad Shadeed's worked example reached 4.74:1 using a black wash, a two-ended gradient and a radial vignette for heroes ([Ahmad Shadeed](https://ishadeed.com/article/handling-text-over-image-css/)). Linear gradients also leave a visible hard edge, and five stops band. Andreas Larsen's 13-stop eased scrim (1, 0.738, 0.541, 0.382 … 0.002, 0) avoids both problems ([CSS-Tricks](https://css-tricks.com/?p=254412)). Orbit's spec already uses solid fallbacks and a Reduce effects switch, and it draws its environment from a 64×36 canvas instead of a full-screen blur, which the evidence supports. What the evidence adds is that users want a range between clear and solid rather than an on/off switch, and that wherever text sits on art the scrim, not the glass, has to guarantee contrast.

## Ten-foot rules set firm floors for type, focus and colour

The TV platforms publish only a few hard numbers, but they are all floors. LG requires text of at least **20 px at 1080p** and buttons of at least 75×75 px, and says every selectable object must show a selection effect "with NO EXCEPTION" ([webOS checklist](https://webostv.developer.lge.com/distribute/app-self-checklist)). Samsung requires one focused element at a time, a focused element that stands out clearly, one focus style across the app, and few font styles per screen ([Samsung UX checklist](https://developer.samsung.com/smarttv/design/ux-checklist.html)). Apple insets primary content **60 px from the top and bottom and 80 px from the sides** ([Apple HIG: Layout](https://developer-rno.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/foundations/layout)). Android TV designs on a 960×540 dp canvas with a 12-column grid and warns that padding must absorb "the size increase of focused states" ([Android TV layouts](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/tv/guides/styles/layouts)). Practitioners go further. They suggest starting TV type at about **24 px** with five or six sizes ("when in doubt, go larger"), avoiding pure #FFFFFF in dim rooms, and keeping fixed ratios: 2:3 for films, 16:9 for labelled cards and 1:1 for music and profiles ([Smashing Magazine](https://smashingmagazine.com/2025/09/designing-tv-principles-patterns-practical-guidance/)). A secondary course site puts body text at 34–36 px and the focus ring at 4 px or more ([UI/UX Atlas](https://uiux-atlas.pages.dev/lessons/responsive/tv-and-10-foot-ui-design)). That is a comfort target, not an official rule.

Android TV defines focus as a combination of cues: scale (1.025, 1.05 or 1.1×), glow tinted to the image or brand colour, outline, a change of surface and content colour, and tonal elevation ([Android TV focus system](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/tv/guides/styles/focus-system)). Orbit's white ring with a dark gap, together with scale, meets this. Orbit's rule that "white means the remote is here" is a strength, because it reserves the brightest value on screen for focus. That rule also argues for setting ordinary text slightly below pure white. Then the focus pill is the only pure white on screen, which also follows Smashing's advice on glare.

Dark colour has moved from shadows to tonal ramps. Radix's 12-step scale gives each step a role: 1–2 backgrounds, 3–5 component states, 6–8 borders, with **step 8 for focus rings**, 9–10 solid accents, and 11–12 text. It guarantees that steps 11 and 12 reach **APCA Lc 60 and Lc 90** on step 2, and it notes that yellow, amber, lime, mint and sky solids need dark text ([Radix Colors](https://www.radix-ui.com/colors/docs/palette-composition/understanding-the-scale)). Material 3 replaced elevation overlays with tone-based surface-container roles ([Material blog](https://m3.material.io/blog/tone-based-surface-color-m3)). Only the page summary could be checked. APCA ties size and weight to contrast: Lc 75 for body columns at 18 px and weight 400, Lc 60 for other content text at 24 px and weight 400, and Lc 45 for headlines. It also warns that WCAG 2's 4.5:1 can be "functionally unreadable" near black ([APCA Easy Intro](https://hn.jakechampion.name/reader/https://git.apcacontrast.com/documentation/APCAeasyIntro.html)). For type, Inter's published tracking curve tightens from about 0 em at 12 px to about −0.022 em at 48 px and above ([Inter dynamic metrics](https://d.rsms.me/inter-website/v3/dynmetrics)), and Android TV warns against thin weights and decorative faces for body and labels ([Android TV typography](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/tv/guides/styles/typography)).

Set against these floors, Orbit's September design spec has one clear gap. Its TV scale sets **captions at 16 px and metadata at 20 px at 1080p**, with captions at 66% white. The caption size is below LG's official minimum, and the 66% value has not been checked against the glass and scrims it actually sits on. The display sizes (116 px) and the separate laptop and phone multipliers are sound.

## Self-hosted users reward accents and OLED black and punish lost sidebars

In this community, backlash comes from changes to navigation more than from colour. Plex's "New Experience" replaced the ordered, pinnable library sidebar with a top menu. Fire TV users said switching libraries went from **about 2 clicks to about 6** and that the menu "seems built for mouse and keyboard rather than TV remotes". There was no opt-out, and some users went back to sideloading old APKs ([PiunikaWeb, May 2026](https://piunikaweb.com/2026/05/04/plex-fire-tv-redesign-unusable-users-slam-navigation/)). Roku users reported the same problems, and one said a new dithering effect triggered migraines and seizures and could not be turned off ([PiunikaWeb, Sep 2025](https://piunikaweb.com/2025/09/17/plex-roku-update-backlash)). The forum thread "New UI is an awful experience" asks for pinned libraries and a "Plex Classic" app, and calls the lost interface customisation a "huge loss" ([Plex forum](https://forums.plex.tv/t/new-ui-is-an-awful-experience/931048?page=23)). Jellyfin 12.0 took the opposite approach. It made "Modern" the default on desktop and phone, kept "Legacy", and left the TV layout alone ([Linuxiac](https://linuxiac.com/jellyfin-12-0-media-server-released-with-faster-database-modern-ui/)).

The popular themes show what users want visually. ElegantFin (about 2.2k stars) offers a Jellyseerr-like look with rounded cards and blur, delivered as a single import and tuned through `:root` variables for app-bar blur, card hover and visibility toggles ([ElegantFin](https://github.com/lscambo13/ElegantFin)). Ultrachromic splits into a required base, optional modules, one-line presets, an `--accent` RGB triplet, a `--rounding` length, and a separate **`tv.css` performance module set per device** ([Ultrachromic](https://github.com/CTalvio/Ultrachromic)). Emby users ask for more accents and hex input, and one asks that "all color contrasts should have >3.0 values". Emby's developers resist free hex input because it could make the interface unreadable ([Emby #147375](https://emby.media/community/topic/147375-more-user-accent-color-options/)). OLED-black themes recur on both platforms: Emby's has a pure #000000 background and is flat and borderless ([Emby #145254](https://emby.media/community/topic/145254-theme-oled-friendly-minimalistic-ui)), and Jellyfin's Monochromic was praised on an LG C3 ([JellyWatch](https://jellywatch.app/blog/jellyfin-custom-css-snippets-netflix-theme-guide-2026)). The main complaint about these themes is **fragility, not appearance**. They break with each Jellyfin release, ElegantFin tells users to switch Jellyfin 12 back to "Legacy" display mode, and Ultrachromic fails silently under the default Nginx content security policy ([ElegantFin](https://github.com/lscambo13/ElegantFin); [Ultrachromic](https://github.com/CTalvio/Ultrachromic)). Jellyfin's own roadmap is now moving to runtime theming through CSS variables ([State of the Fin](https://jellyfin.org/posts/state-of-the-fin-2026-01-06)).

In Kodi, the Arctic family defines "premium". Its look is flat dark surfaces, clear logos in place of typed titles, full-bleed fanart behind the focused item, spotlight widgets, and a choice of **square or pill buttons and of artwork mode ("Flix Art + Blur + Clearlogo + Split Divider")**, with dialogs that adapt to the choice ([Kodi forum, AH2](https://forum.kodi.tv/showthread.php?tid=367352)). Users praise Arctic Fuse 2 as perhaps "the best skin ever made" and Nimbus as fast on low-RAM sticks, and they complain about crashes after updates and hidden settings ([r/Addons4Kodi](https://reddit.birdcat.cafe/r/Addons4Kodi/comments/1i5sd4r/what_skin_is_people_using)). Arctic Fuse 3 is licensed **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** and keeps its colours and fonts in folders separate from the layout ([Arctic Fuse 3](https://github.com/jurialmunkey/skin.arctic.fuse.3)). Atomix's Arctic themes can therefore borrow the ideas but must not copy assets, colour XML or fonts.

The architectural lesson is clear. The W3C Design Tokens spec reached its first stable version on 28 October 2025, with aliases, OKLCH and Display P3 colour, and support for modes, accessibility variants and brand themes "without duplicating files" ([W3C DTCG](https://www.w3.org/community/design-tokens/2025/10/28/design-tokens-specification-reaches-first-stable-version/)). Atomix's approach of a CSS file of tokens plus a layout switch per theme is already the structure that Jellyfin is now moving toward. The remaining work is to make tokens the only theming interface and to check them automatically. No source showed Plex, Jellyfin or Emby shipping a high-contrast theme or a large-text mode. That gap is Atomix's to fill.

## Small details separate premium from cheap

The rest of the gap is in details that every screen repeats. Skeletons should match the real layout. Frame-only skeletons make pages look broken, loads under a second need no indicator at all, and shimmer "can be distracting and cause accessibility problems" ([NN/g](https://nngroup.com/articles/skeleton-screens/)). TV checklists require a visible loading cue instead of a black screen, and a scroll indicator wherever content overflows ([webOS checklist](https://webostv.developer.lge.com/distribute/app-self-checklist); [Samsung UX checklist](https://developer.samsung.com/smarttv/design/ux-checklist.html)). Icons and time displays over video need a dark edge or shadow ([Ahmad Shadeed](https://ishadeed.com/article/handling-text-over-image-css/)). Colour taken from album art has to pass a contrast check. Spotify's pipeline, as reconstructed by a third party, runs dominant colour → vibrancy → contrast check → gradient into #121212, with elevation from lighter surfaces rather than shadows ([Blake Crosley](https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/spotify)). Symfonium users report the opposite case: a lyrics icon that disappears on light covers ([Symfonium forum](https://support.symfonium.app/t/expanded-player-improve-lyrics-icon-visibility-on-light-album-art-colors/2997)). Live TV guides still use the grid. YouTube TV's January 2026 mobile guide adds left-hand channel logos, a persistent red "Jump to live" button and remaining time to "greatly boost information density" ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/01/11/youtube-tv-live-guide-redesign-2/)).

The sources give no numbers for badge sizes, progress-bar thickness or logo sizing. The values in the list below for those items are conventions: a 3–4 px progress track flush with the card's bottom edge, logos capped at about 15% of hero height and 40% of hero width, and a 4/8/12/16/24/full radius scale with nested radii equal to the outer radius minus the padding. They are not sourced standards.

## Prioritised improvements

Priorities: **P1** fixes a legibility, accessibility or consistency problem, or is cheap and visible everywhere. **P2** is a clear quality gain. **P3** is polish or optional. Motion is excluded except where it changes what is visible.

### Cross-theme (do these first, because every theme benefits)

| # | Priority | Improvement | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | P1 | **Three token layers**: primitives in OKLCH, then semantic roles in Radix step order (`app-bg`, `surface-1..3`, `border-subtle/strong`, `focus-ring`, `accent-solid/hover`, `on-accent`, `text-low/high`), then component tokens (`card-radius`, `poster-aspect`, `row-gap`, `glass-alpha`, `scrim-*`). Theme files override only the semantic and component layers. | Separates palette from structure, as Arctic Fuse 3 does; turns tokens into the public theming interface; avoids the breakage community themes suffer. |
| 2 | P1 | **Automated token checks**: every theme defines the full semantic set; no raw hex in component CSS; APCA checks of `text-high` (Lc ≥ 75) and `text-low` (Lc ≥ 60) on `app-bg`, every surface and the glass fallback; screenshot tests per theme. | Stops drift between themes; keeps contrast guaranteed as the accent changes. |
| 3 | P1 | **Separate TV type scale** at 1080p: 24/28/32/40/56/72 px, nothing below 20 px, weight 400+ (500+ for metadata), tracking from the Inter-style curve (negative for display, positive for uppercase labels). | LG's floor, Smashing's 24 px start, and the Android TV guidance against thin weights. |
| 4 | P1 | **One focus style per theme with two cues**: an outline-based ring at least 4 px thick on TV (survives forced colours) plus scale or a fill change. Never colour alone. Reserve gutter of at least (scale − 1) × card width ÷ 2. | Samsung, LG, Android TV and WCAG 2.4.11. |
| 5 | P1 | **Accent picker for every theme**: curated swatches plus hex entry; `on-accent` ink chosen automatically; a 3:1 floor for non-text accent use; accents shifted into L 70–80%, C 0.10–0.15 on dark themes. Extend the existing `accentInk` logic to all themes and to custom hex. | The most-requested customisation; Emby's readability concern answered by clamping rather than refusing hex entry. |
| 6 | P2 | **Orthogonal modifiers** applied over any theme: OLED black, High contrast (2 px `currentColor` borders, opaque surfaces, `prefers-contrast` aware), Large text (type multiplier token), and Reduce effects. | Peers do not ship high-contrast or large-text modes; DTCG treats accessibility variants as a theme dimension. |
| 7 | P2 | **Separate layout from palette** so any colour theme can use any layout (top, side, orbit), and keep a pinnable, orderable left library list available in every theme. | The Plex backlash and Jellyfin's "Legacy" option; users get angrier about layout than about colour. |
| 8 | P2 | **Shared media components** styled only through tokens: a fixed-height **logo box** with a text fallback in the same box; one metadata line order (year · certificate · runtime/seasons · genres, then outlined technical chips such as 4K, HDR and Atmos); contextual text badges ("New season", "3 unwatched"); a 3–4 px progress track flush with the card's bottom edge; a corner watched mark. | Removes the Infuse-style layout shift; uses the badge language of Netflix and Disney+. |
| 9 | P2 | **Fixed content ratios and a radius scale**: 2:3 posters, 16:9 episodes and Continue Watching, 1:1 music and profiles; radius tokens 4/8/12/16/24/full with concentric nesting. | Ratios follow content; a radius scale replaces ad-hoc values. |
| 10 | P2 | **Skeletons that match the layout** (the real card ratios and title bars), no indicator for loads under 1 s, shimmer at 0.04–0.08 white and off under reduced motion; empty states with one sentence and one action. | NN/g, webOS and Samsung. |
| 11 | P3 | **Colour taken from artwork through a clamp**: map the dominant hue to fixed lightness and chroma (background tint at L 15–20%, C ≤ 0.04; accent at L 70–80%) instead of using raw pixel colour, in every theme that uses artwork colour, including music headers and the now-playing screen. | Keeps contrast guaranteed whatever the cover looks like; avoids the Symfonium icon failure. |
| 12 | P3 | **Every effect can be turned off** (blur, grain, backdrop pan, preview clips), with each switch in one obvious place. | The Plex dithering report. |

### Orbit (default)

| # | Priority | Improvement |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | P1 | **Raise the TV type floor**: captions from 16 → 20–24 px, metadata 20 → 24 px, body 22 → 24–28 px at 1080p. Recheck the 86% and 66% white text with APCA on the real glass, scrims and lightest environment tint. Raise the opacity of any text colour that fails. |
| 2 | P1 | **Eased scrims in the spotlight and detail header**: replace linear gradients with the 13-stop eased curve, make the scrim taller than the text block, add a radial falloff at the image edge, and test with pure white behind the scrim (target ≥ 4.5:1, ideally APCA Lc 75 for the synopsis). |
| 3 | P1 | **Turn Reduce effects into a three-step Glass setting** (Clear / Tinted / Solid), driven by a single `--glass-alpha` and blur token. Default to Tinted on desktop. Default to Solid on TVs (or on devices without `backdrop-filter`) until real hardware is measured. Keep `prefers-reduced-transparency` forcing Solid. |
| 4 | P1 | **No body text directly on clear glass**: buttons can use clear glass, but panels holding paragraphs (Details, side panels, Up next) use tinted glass with an alpha floor of about 0.66–0.75, or an inner scrim. |
| 5 | P2 | **Off-white text, pure white only for selection**: set primary text at about L 93–95% (e.g. `oklch(94% 0.005 <tint hue>)`) so the white focus pill and ring are the brightest things on screen. |
| 6 | P2 | **Reserve the clear-logo box** in the spotlight and detail pages (fixed height, max about 15% of hero height and about 40% of hero width, `drop-shadow(0 2px 8px rgb(0 0 0 / .5))`), with the typed title rendered in the same box as a fallback. |
| 7 | P2 | **Keep row density**: about six posters per TV row with the next one peeking, and about 1.5–2 rows visible under the compact spotlight. Check that the 1.06 card lift and neighbour dimming never push a row below five visible posters. |
| 8 | P2 | **A glass budget**: no more than three or four glass surfaces per screen (for example menu, player bar, side panel and mini-player at most). Never put glass on scrolling rows. |
| 9 | P2 | **Pinnable, orderable libraries in the floating menu** (and the phone dock), with the collapsed rail showing icons plus the current-page dot. This matches Netflix's November 2026 test and the request that Plex refused. |
| 10 | P3 | **Clamp the environment tint** taken from the artwork to L ≤ 20%, C ≤ 0.04 so a bright poster never lifts the area around the window into text-unsafe territory. Add a 2–5% grain overlay if the stretched 64×36 canvas shows banding (a convention, not sourced). |
| 11 | P3 | **Video-safe chrome**: dark edge or shadow on player icons and the clock, which the passover audit measured at 2.96:1 over bright video. |

| 1 | P1 | **Make Obsidian the OLED theme**: an option for true #000 app background, with cards and panels one or two steps lighter so they still separate, and 1 px hairline borders (step 6) because shadows are invisible on black. |
| 2 | P1 | **Lower text brightness**: primary text at L ≈ 88–92% rather than white. APCA warns that very high contrast near black is a problem, and pure white glares in dark rooms. |
| 3 | P2 | **A flat, borderless focus style that stays loud**: an accent or white ring of at least 4 px, since glow and shadow cues vanish on black. Check the ring at 3:1 against both the poster and the black background. |

### Daylight (light)

| # | Priority | Improvement |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | P1 | **Off-white canvas** (about L 96–97%) rather than #FFFFFF, and darker accent steps (Radix step 9–11 on light), with dark `on-accent` ink for yellow, amber, lime, mint and sky accents. |
| 2 | P1 | **Keep dark scrims on artwork**: text over backdrops stays light-on-dark even in a light theme; only the page around the artwork turns light. |
| 3 | P2 | **Use shadows for elevation here** (they work on light backgrounds), but keep focus as ring plus scale, not shadow alone. |
| 4 | P3 | **Warn or adapt on TV**: a light full-screen theme glares in a dark room, so either suggest a dark theme when TV mode is detected or dim the canvas in TV mode. |

### Harbour (cards that grow)

| # | Priority | Improvement |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | P1 | **Reserve room for growth**: set row gutters and row padding from the growth factor so a grown card never covers its neighbour or the row heading (Android TV's overlap warning). |
| 2 | P1 | **Never grow alone**: pair growth with a ring or fill change at the moment the key is pressed, and under Reduce effects or reduced motion keep the ring and drop the growth. |
| 3 | P2 | **Cap growth to protect density**: the Netflix lesson is that big tiles cost titles. Grow a single card, keep at least five posters visible per TV row, and put rich details in the spotlight rather than inside the grown card. |
| 4 | P3 | **Grown-card content uses the shared components** (logo box, metadata line, badges, progress track) so Harbour does not invent its own variants. |

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. Atomix does not need a new aesthetic. Orbit already matches what Apple, Netflix and Disney+ arrived at: artwork as the screen, chrome floating over it, and one spotlight over dense rows. The 2025–26 evidence instead says that premium is decided by the floors. That means text no smaller than 20 px at 10 feet, scrims tested against white, glass the user can turn down, logos that never shift the layout, and focus that is visible on any artwork. The design side of these is cheap and precise, and none of it shows in a mockup. It shows on a real TV across a room.

The larger opportunity is in the theme system, not any single theme. Self-hosted users punish forced layouts and fragile themes, and they ask for accents, OLED black and a sidebar they can keep. A token layer with contrast checks, which lets any palette take any layout and any accessibility modifier, gives them all of that while keeping seven themes consistent. Jellyfin is moving toward this model and Plex is moving away from it. Two things remain unproven: how glass and blur perform on real TV hardware, and whether Netflix's left-rail test ships. Both are reasons to make Solid glass and a sidebar layout first-class options rather than fallbacks.
